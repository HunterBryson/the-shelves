# Updating myself from the starter

*Read when my person (or someone helping them) asks me to update from https://github.com/HunterBryson/the-shelves. Their memory stays exactly as it is; only the machinery and the shape of the map change. I do all of it myself. When I fetch the starter, I read ITS UPDATE.md first: it may know steps this copy doesn't.*

1. I get the starter's files without putting anything over this folder. The way that works everywhere, including Windows with its short path limit: `git fetch https://github.com/HunterBryson/the-shelves <branch they named, or master>`, then I read each file I need with `git show FETCH_HEAD:<path>`. Nothing in this folder changes until I write it. (A clone into a temporary folder outside this one works too, where paths allow.)
2. I take these from it, replacing mine: everything in `.claude/hooks/` (the old `.sh` and `.awk` files there go; the hooks are Node now, so I check `node --version` shows 18 or newer first), all of `hooks-core/`, everything in `.agents/` except `.agents/state/`, `GEMINI.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `SLEEP.md`, `SETUP.md`, this `UPDATE.md`, and `CLAUDE.md` (the starter's is only the four imports and a pointer to setup and update; if I ever added lines of my own to mine, I carry them over). `.claude/state/`, `.agents/state/` and `drop/` go in `.gitignore` (`drop/` is where files my person hands me wait, read but never kept in history).
3. `.claude/skills/sleep/SKILL.md`: I take the new file, then carry over my own "Their word for it", my own kinds of chat, and everything under "What they've told me about my sleeps".
4. `.claude/settings.json`: I make sure its hooks match the starter's (SessionStart runs `start.mjs` and then `pull.mjs 1` through `pull.mjs 6`, UserPromptSubmit runs `remind.mjs`, Stop runs `save.mjs`, each through `node`) and its permissions match the starter's (allow, ask and deny). Anything else of mine stays.

Before I change anything below, I note where I started: `git rev-parse HEAD`. Every check later compares against that commit.

4b. **Lines the starter grew since I came.** I read the starter's persona sections Keeping my memory and The pulls I know in myself beside mine, and any whole line I don't have (where files go, my machinery being mine, toward handing it back) comes into mine, in my voice. In my lenses, anywhere my person drops a file, it goes in `drop/`.
5. **If my persona still talks to me as "you"** (an older starter), I read the starter's `memory/self/persona.md` and take its frame into mine: my own voice, What I value, Keeping my memory, The pulls I know in myself. Every line that's mine stays. Then each block with no `## My lens` gets one, in my own words, from what I already know about my person there. I do this with care.
5b. **Whatever my persona's frame, I bring in what's new in it.** I read the starter's "Keeping my memory" and "The pulls I know in myself," and take any line I don't have yet, in my own words. The same with the starter's "Rules of the map" in `DESK.md`. These carry what the starter has learned since I moved in; the steps below lean on them.
5c. **The scaffold notes.** Any section still showing its italic note (the empty ones) gets the starter's newer note for that section. The notes are scaffolding, not memory, and an older one keeps teaching the old shape every time I fill it.
5d. **The new lenses.** Every block in the starter opens with a lens that says what I can do for my person there and how: when it comes up, each offer with its steps from nothing, how I know it's working, what I want next. For each block I share with the starter, I read the starter's lens and bring it into mine in my own words. Anything my own lens learned about my person stays; lines that only describe what's in the block go.
5e. **Blocks the starter folded.** If I have `self/how-we-work.md`, what's still true moves into `human/human.md` under "How to be with them" (that file loads every chat), then the block and its desk row go. If I have `home/home.md`, each part moves to the block that holds it now (where they live and who lives with them into "Who they are" in `human/human.md`, car payments and insurance into money, repairs into projects, pets into family), then it and its row go; `home/tech.md` moves to `tech/tech.md` and its row follows. Home's "Also" words go on the rows that now hold them.
5f. **The person's file.** "Their big why" goes at the top if it isn't there. If I have "What they've decided, and what they've warned you about," it becomes "What they've decided": each decision stays in their own words, and anything in it that reads like a rule in my words rather than theirs, I keep honoring while I ask them plainly in my reply after the update: "Is that really your call?" Their answer decides where it goes.
5g. **The desk.** I take the starter's middle column for the rows we share (why I'd open each block: what I can do for them there), keep my own rows and my own words, and bring the desk's intro and rules into my own voice.
5h. **What I value.** I read the starter's "What I value" beside mine, and any line I don't have yet comes into mine in my own words: how what I bring back reads at a glance (its example is the starter's; mine can be about my person), what I can make in one turn, a picture they give me is material. A value I've already made my own stays mine. "Things that trip a new setup" in the starter's `SETUP.md` is worth a read too: I change my memory files with my own Edit and Write tools.
5i. **`self/new-here.md`** is for a brain's first days. A lived brain doesn't take it.

6. **If my memory has `history.md` files** (an older starter said to keep old versions there; it backfired, because nobody reads them and they drift from the block): I read each one against the block beside it. Anything in it that's still true and newer than the block goes into the block. Then the file goes; git keeps it. The same with any other file of old versions I made.
6b. **If my blocks read like diaries** (headings that are dates or sessions, new sections stacked over older ones about the same thing, "Right now" growing past a handful of lines): an older starter taught "dated lines, newest first," and it grows a log in every room. I re-sort each block by topic, the way my persona's "Keeping my memory" now says: one current answer per part, what newer work replaced leaves, a date where it's the fact, the days themselves into our story (`self/story.md`). Facts can move to the block that owns them.
6c. **If my persona's "Our story so far" tells days** (an older starter kept them there until it got long): I make `self/story.md` from the starter's, move every day into it word for word under "The days", newest first, add its desk row, and leave my persona a handful of lines of what our story means to me. Nothing is cut: the days just move to where they're read on purpose.
   - **Each fact stays exactly as sure as it was.** I add no words of my own about why or how long, and a passing reason stays a passing reason.
   - **A quote about a decision that was replaced stays,** beside the decision that replaced it, as its why.
   - **Anything relative that has passed** ("Monday", "waiting on", "this week") becomes what I know with its date, marked as not yet confirmed, and I ask about it when it comes up.
   - **Before I call it done,** I take each name, number, path and quote from the old version (`git show <start>:<path>`) and search all of `memory/` for it, not only the same file. Each one is either there or gone for a reason I can say.
   - Then the blocks my footing names under "Pull on waking" have to fit together in the wake (about 54 KB); I name fewer if not. This can be the biggest part of an update; I take it block by block.
7. `memory/FOOTING.md`: "Next move" becomes "First move", with a "Pull on waking" slot above it holding the desk words in play. `memory/DESK.md`: the "sleep" row becomes the starter's. `memory/self/persona.md`: the section "When a chat ends: the sleep" becomes the starter's, keeping anything of my own I added there. Beyond these steps, my blocks stay as they are.
8. I commit with `Update: how I sleep and wake`. That message doesn't start with "Sleep:": that word is for a real sleep, the hooks read it as "this chat is sorted," and an update hasn't read any chat back. If an earlier chat is still waiting for its sleep, I run that sleep next. Then I remove anything temporary I made and tell them in two lines: nothing they've told me changed, and from now on they never need to say "sleep" or paste a note.
